## Executive Summary

This systematic literature review examines 10 key papers on latent reasoning in natural language processing published between July 2023 and July 2025. The reviewed literature reveals a rapidly evolving research area focused on enabling language models to perform reasoning operations in latent or continuous spaces, rather than solely through explicit natural language chain-of-thought. Key themes include: (1) continuous latent space reasoning, (2) implicit chain-of-thought internalization, (3) pause tokens and test-time computation, (4) multi-hop reasoning mechanisms, and (5) hybrid representations combining latent and text tokens.

## Annotated Bibliography

### 1. Continuous Latent Space Reasoning

**Hao, S., Sukhbaatar, S., Su, D., Li, X., Hu, Z., Weston, J., & Tian, Y. (2024).** Training Large Language Models to Reason in a Continuous Latent Space. *arXiv preprint arXiv:2412.06769*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06769v4

**Citations:** 14

This paper introduces Coconut (Chain of Continuous Thought), a paradigm that utilizes the last hidden state of LLMs as "continuous thought" representations fed back as next input embeddings directly in continuous space. The approach enables breadth-first search during planning rather than committing to single deterministic paths as in traditional CoT. Coconut outperforms CoT on logical reasoning tasks requiring substantial search, achieving better accuracy-efficiency tradeoffs.

**Key Contribution:** First formal framework for reasoning entirely in continuous latent space without decoding to words.

---

**Cheng, J., & Van Durme, B. (2024).** Compressed Chain of Thought: Efficient Reasoning Through Dense Representations. *arXiv preprint arXiv:2412.13171*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13171v1

**Citations:** 6

Proposes Compressed Chain-of-Thought (CCoT), generating contentful continuous contemplation tokens of variable sequence length as compressed representations of explicit reasoning chains. The method applies to off-the-shelf decoder models and enables adaptive reasoning improvements by controlling contemplation token count.

**Key Contribution:** Variable-length compressed reasoning representations for efficient inference.

---

**Su, D., Zhu, H., Xu, Y., Jiao, J., Tian, Y., & Zheng, Q. (2025).** Token Assorted: Mixing Latent and Text Tokens for Improved Language Model Reasoning. *arXiv preprint arXiv:2502.03275*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03275v2

**Citations:** 5

Introduces hybrid reasoning representations where initial steps are abstracted using latent discrete tokens generated by VQ-VAE, significantly reducing reasoning trace length. Training with randomly mixed latent and text tokens enables fast adaptation. The approach consistently outperforms baselines on logical and mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

**Key Contribution:** Hybrid latent-text token mixing for computational efficiency.

---

### 2. Implicit Chain-of-Thought and Internalization

**Deng, Y., Choi, Y., & Shieber, S. (2024).** From Explicit CoT to Implicit CoT: Learning to Internalize CoT Step by Step. *arXiv preprint arXiv:2405.14838*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14838v1

**Citations:** 9

Proposes gradual removal of intermediate CoT steps through finetuning, allowing models to internalize reasoning processes. GPT-2 Small achieved 99% accuracy on 9-by-9 multiplication (versus 4-by-4 with standard training), and Mistral 7B reached over 50% accuracy on GSM8K without producing intermediate steps.

**Key Contribution:** Progressive CoT internalization maintaining performance without explicit steps.

---

**Deng, Y., Prasad, K., Fernandez, R., Smolensky, P., Chaudhary, V., & Shieber, S. (2023).** Implicit Chain of Thought Reasoning via Knowledge Distillation. *arXiv preprint arXiv:2311.01460*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01460v1

**Citations:** 6

Explores implicit reasoning through internal hidden states distilled from teacher models trained on explicit CoT. Reasoning occurs "vertically" among hidden states across layers rather than "horizontally" through intermediate words. Enables solving previously unsolvable tasks at speeds comparable to no-CoT inference.

**Key Contribution:** Vertical reasoning through hidden state distillation.

---

### 3. Pause Tokens and Test-Time Computation

**Goyal, S., Ji, Z., Rawat, A. S., Menon, A. K., Kumar, S., & Nagarajan, V. (2023).** Think before you speak: Training Language Models With Pause Tokens. *arXiv preprint arXiv:2310.02226*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02226v3

**Citations:** 7

Introduces learnable pause tokens appended to input prefixes, allowing models to manipulate extra hidden vectors before outputting tokens. A 1B model showed gains on 8 of 9 tasks: 18% EM improvement on SQuAD, 8% on CommonSenseQA, and 1% on GSM8k when both pretrained and finetuned with delays.

**Key Contribution:** Delayed next-token prediction paradigm for enhanced computation.

---

**Muennighoff, N., Yang, Z., Shi, W., Li, X. L., Fei-Fei, L., Hajishirzi, H., Zettlemoyer, L., Liang, P., Candès, E., & Hashimoto, T. (2025).** s1: Simple test-time scaling. *arXiv preprint arXiv:2501.19393*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19393v3

**Citations:** 6

Presents budget forcing to control test-time compute by terminating or lengthening thinking processes. After supervised finetuning on curated s1K dataset (1,000 questions with reasoning traces), s1-32B exceeds o1-preview by up to 27% on MATH and AIME24. Scaling with budget forcing improves AIME24 from 50% to 57%.

**Key Contribution:** Simple test-time scaling with budget forcing for reasoning enhancement.

---

**DeepSeek-AI et al. (2025).** DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning. *arXiv preprint arXiv:2501.12948*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948v2

**Citations:** 10

Demonstrates that LLM reasoning abilities can be incentivized through pure reinforcement learning without human-labeled reasoning trajectories. The RL framework facilitates emergent self-reflection, verification, and dynamic strategy adaptation. Trained models surpass counterparts trained via supervised learning on mathematics, coding, and STEM tasks.

**Key Contribution:** Pure RL-based reasoning capability development without human demonstrations.

---

### 4. Multi-Hop Latent Reasoning Analysis

**Yang, S., Gribovskaya, E., Kassner, N., Geva, M., & Riedel, S. (2024).** Do Large Language Models Latently Perform Multi-Hop Reasoning? *arXiv preprint arXiv:2402.16837*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16837v2

**Citations:** 9

Investigates whether LLMs latently perform multi-hop reasoning on complex prompts. Found strong evidence for latent multi-hop reasoning in certain relation types (>80% of prompts), though utilization is highly contextual. Clear scaling trends observed for first hop but not second hop, suggesting challenges in deeper reasoning chains.

**Key Contribution:** Empirical evidence and analysis of latent multi-hop reasoning pathways.

---

**Biran, E., Gottesman, D., Yang, S., Geva, M., & Globerson, A. (2024).** Hopping Too Late: Exploring the Limitations of Large Language Models on Multi-Hop Queries. *arXiv preprint arXiv:2406.12775*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12775v2

**Citations:** 5

Analyzes internal computations showing bridge entity resolution occurs in early layers, with two-hop queries solved in later layers. Proposes "back-patching" method where later layer representations are patched to earlier layers, correcting 66% of previously incorrect cases. Reveals that later layers sometimes lack needed functionality for correct answers.

**Key Contribution:** Layer-by-layer analysis of latent reasoning and back-patching intervention method.

## Thematic Synthesis

### Emergent Patterns

1. **Shift from Explicit to Implicit:** Multiple papers (Deng et al., 2023; Deng et al., 2024) demonstrate that explicit CoT can be internalized, maintaining performance while reducing inference overhead.

2. **Continuous vs. Discrete:** The field is exploring both continuous latent spaces (Hao et al., 2024; Cheng & Van Durme, 2024) and discrete latent tokens (Su et al., 2025), suggesting complementary approaches.

3. **Test-Time Computation:** Pause tokens (Goyal et al., 2023) and budget forcing (Muennighoff et al., 2025) show that allocating additional computation time during inference improves reasoning without architectural changes.

4. **Reinforcement Learning:** DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025) demonstrates RL can develop reasoning capabilities without human-annotated trajectories, representing a paradigm shift.

5. **Internal Mechanisms:** Yang et al. (2024) and Biran et al. (2024) provide crucial insights into how multi-hop reasoning actually occurs within transformer layers.

### Research Gaps

- **Scalability:** Most approaches tested on specific tasks; generalizability across domains needs investigation
- **Interpretability:** Latent reasoning is inherently less interpretable than explicit CoT
- **Optimal Balance:** The ideal mix of latent vs. explicit reasoning remains unclear
- **Layer Dynamics:** Deeper understanding needed of how reasoning propagates across transformer layers

---

## Conclusions

The literature from 2023-2025 reveals latent reasoning as a vibrant research direction addressing fundamental limitations of explicit chain-of-thought approaches. Key advances include continuous latent space reasoning, CoT internalization, test-time computation scaling, and mechanistic understanding of multi-hop reasoning. The convergence of these approaches suggests a future where language models reason through hybrid representations combining the efficiency of latent computation with the interpretability of natural language when needed.

Reinforcement learning emerges as a promising direction for developing reasoning capabilities without human supervision, while mechanistic analyses provide crucial insights for improving model architectures. The field appears poised to move beyond the CoT paradigm toward more sophisticated reasoning mechanisms operating in both continuous and discrete latent spaces.
