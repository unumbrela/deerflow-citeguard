# Systematic Literature Review: Latent Reasoning

**Date**: 2026-10-26
**Papers surveyed**: 10
**Scope**: "latent reasoning", cs.CL category, published between 2023-07-08 and 2025-07-07
**Citation format**: APA 7th edition

## Executive Summary

The literature on latent reasoning in large language models reveals a rapidly evolving field focused on moving beyond explicit, language-based chain-of-thought reasoning toward more efficient, internalized reasoning mechanisms. Surveyed papers collectively demonstrate that LLMs can perform sophisticated multi-hop reasoning through hidden representations rather than explicit token generation, with emerging approaches leveraging continuous latent spaces, compressed representations, and hybrid token mixing. The field shows strong convergence around the idea that much of the text in traditional chain-of-thought serves textual coherence rather than core reasoning, enabling significant efficiency gains through latent reasoning paradigms. However, challenges remain in consistently utilizing latent reasoning across different task types and ensuring reliable multi-hop traversal in later model layers.

## Methodology

This review surveyed 10 arXiv papers retrieved on 2026-10-26 using the query "latent reasoning", filtered to category cs.CL, published between 2023-07-08 and 2025-07-07. Papers were sorted by relevance through citation expansion and co-citation analysis, with the top 10 selected for inclusion. Metadata extraction (research question, methodology, key findings, limitations) was performed by language-model agents, with cross-paper synthesis performed by the lead agent.

**Screening flow**: 50 papers identified by search, 83 added from reference expansion, 30 shortlisted, 30 screened, 10 included (6 from reference expansion).

**Limitations of this review**: arXiv preprints are not peer-reviewed; some included papers may not reflect their final published form. Coverage is limited to arXiv — papers published directly in venues without arXiv preprints are not represented.

## Themes

### Theme 1: Continuous Latent Space Reasoning

A prominent direction involves training LLMs to operate in continuous latent spaces rather than discrete language spaces. Hao et al. (2024) introduced Coconut (Chain of Continuous Thought), which utilizes the last hidden state of the LLM as a reasoning representation fed back as input embeddings directly in continuous space. This enables breadth-first search reasoning patterns rather than deterministic chain-of-thought paths, with continuous thoughts encoding multiple alternative next steps simultaneously. Similarly, Cheng and Van Durme (2024) proposed Compressed Chain-of-Thought (CCoT), generating contentful continuous contemplation tokens as compressed representations of explicit reasoning chains. Both approaches demonstrate that latent reasoning achieves better accuracy-efficiency tradeoffs compared to traditional CoT, with most word tokens primarily ensuring textual coherence rather than essential reasoning.

### Theme 2: Implicit Reasoning Through Hidden States

Multiple papers explore reasoning that occurs vertically among hidden states across layers rather than horizontally through word generation. Deng et al. (2023) demonstrated that implicit reasoning via knowledge distillation enables solving tasks previously not solvable without explicit chain-of-thought, achieving speed comparable to no chain-of-thought reasoning. Yang et al. (2024) provided strong evidence of latent multi-hop reasoning for certain relation types, with the reasoning pathway used in more than 80% of prompts, though utilization remains highly contextual. Deng et al. (2024) further showed that models can internalize explicit CoT steps through gradual removal and finetuning, with GPT-2 Small achieving 99% accuracy on 9-by-9 multiplication—far beyond the 4-by-4 limit of standard training.

### Theme 3: Reinforcement Learning and Test-Time Scaling

Recent work explores incentivizing reasoning capabilities through reinforcement learning and test-time compute control. DeepSeek-AI et al. (2025) demonstrated that pure reinforcement learning can incentivize advanced reasoning patterns including self-reflection, verification, and dynamic strategy adaptation without human-annotated reasoning trajectories, achieving superior performance on mathematics and coding competitions. Muennighoff et al. (2025) developed budget forcing to control test-time compute by forcibly terminating or lengthening the model's thinking process, with their s1-32B model exceeding o1-preview on competition math by up to 27% on MATH and AIME24.

### Theme 4: Hybrid Representation Mixing

Emerging approaches combine latent and text tokens to balance efficiency with performance. Su et al. (2025) proposed partially abstracting initial reasoning steps using latent discrete tokens generated by VQ-VAE to reduce reasoning trace length, consistently outperforming baselines across benchmarks. Their random mixing of latent and text tokens enables fast adaptation to new latent tokens, supporting the finding that many chain-of-thought words support textual coherence rather than core reasoning information.

### Theme 5: Understanding and Patching Reasoning Failures

Several papers investigate why latent reasoning fails and how to patch these failures. Biran et al. (2024) discovered that bridge entity resolution occurs in early layers while second-hop resolution commences in later layers that may lack necessary knowledge, with up to 66% of previously incorrect cases corrected through back-patching. Li et al. (2024) identified that most compositional reasoning failures stem from improperly generated or leveraged implicit reasoning results, with multi-head self-attention modules in middle layers serving as linchpins for accurate reasoning. Their CREME method effectively patches errors via editing these critical MHSA modules.

## Convergences and Disagreements

**Convergences**: The surveyed papers show strong agreement that (1) much of the text in traditional chain-of-thought serves textual coherence rather than core reasoning (Hao et al., 2024; Su et al., 2025; Cheng & Van Durme, 2024), (2) latent reasoning enables significant efficiency gains while maintaining or improving accuracy (Deng et al., 2023; Deng et al., 2024; Hao et al., 2024), and (3) multi-hop reasoning in LLMs is highly contextual, varying substantially across prompt types and relation types (Yang et al., 2024; Biran et al., 2024).

**Disagreements**: While most papers report positive results for latent reasoning, the degree of latent multi-hop reasoning utilization varies considerably. Yang et al. (2024) found evidence for the first hop is substantial but evidence for the second hop and full multi-hop traversal is moderate on average, whereas Hao et al. (2024) report more optimistic results with continuous thoughts encoding multiple alternative steps effectively. This suggests that different latent reasoning paradigms may have varying success rates depending on task complexity and model architecture.

## Gaps and Open Questions

The collective literature reveals several unaddressed gaps:

1. **Scalability to larger models**: Most latent reasoning approaches have been tested primarily on smaller models (GPT-2 Small, Mistral 7B), with limited evidence of scalability to very large models (100B+ parameters).

2. **Task generalization**: The contextual nature of latent reasoning utilization (Yang et al., 2024; Biran et al., 2024) suggests challenges in developing general-purpose latent reasoning systems that work reliably across diverse task domains.

3. **Training efficiency**: While latent reasoning improves inference efficiency, the training costs for methods requiring reinforcement learning (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025) or gradual internalization (Deng et al., 2024) remain unclear.

4. **Interpretability trade-offs**: Moving reasoning to latent spaces improves efficiency but reduces interpretability—a critical tension for safety-critical applications that none of the surveyed papers adequately addresses.

5. **Layer-wise knowledge integration**: The finding that later layers sometimes lack functionality needed for multi-hop resolution (Biran et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024) points to architectural limitations requiring further investigation.

## Per-Paper Annotations

### Biran et al. (2024)

**Research question**: How do LLMs internally compute answers to multi-hop queries, and what causes failures in resolving such queries?

**Methodology**: Analyzed internal computations of transformer-based LLMs on multi-hop queries, discovering bridge entities are resolved in early layers while second-hop resolution occurs in later layers. Proposed novel back-patching analysis where hidden representations from later layers are patched back to earlier layers.

**Key findings**:
- Bridge entity resolution occurs in early layers of the model
- Second-hop resolution commences in later layers, which may lack necessary knowledge
- In up to 66% of previously incorrect cases, a back-patch exists that results in correct answer generation
- Later layers sometimes lack the needed functionality for multi-hop query resolution

**Limitations**: not stated in abstract

### Cheng and Van Durme (2024)

**Research question**: Can chain-of-thought reasoning be made more efficient through compressed, continuous contemplation tokens while maintaining or improving reasoning performance?

**Methodology**: Proposed Compressed Chain-of-Thought (CCoT), a framework generating contentful and continuous contemplation tokens of variable sequence length as compressed representations of explicit reasoning chains, applicable to off-the-shelf decoder language models.

**Key findings**:
- CCoT enables additional reasoning over dense contentful representations to achieve improvements in accuracy
- Reasoning improvements can be adaptively modified on demand by controlling the number of contemplation tokens generated
- Contemplation tokens are compressed representations of explicit reasoning chains
- The method can be applied to off-the-shelf decoder language models without modification

**Limitations**: not stated in abstract

### DeepSeek-AI et al. (2025)

**Research question**: Can the reasoning abilities of large language models be incentivized through pure reinforcement learning without requiring human-annotated reasoning trajectories?

**Methodology**: Proposed a reinforcement learning framework facilitating emergent development of advanced reasoning patterns such as self-reflection, verification, and dynamic strategy adaptation. The trained model's emergent reasoning patterns guide and enhance smaller models' reasoning capabilities.

**Key findings**:
- Reinforcement learning can incentivize reasoning abilities without human-labeled reasoning trajectories
- The RL framework enables emergent development of self-reflection, verification, and dynamic strategy adaptation
- The trained model achieves superior performance on mathematics, coding competitions, and STEM fields compared to counterparts trained via supervised learning
- Emergent reasoning patterns from large-scale models can systematically guide and enhance smaller models' reasoning capabilities

**Limitations**: not stated in abstract

### Deng et al. (2023)

**Research question**: Can language models perform reasoning more effectively using internal hidden states rather than explicit natural language chain-of-thought steps?

**Methodology**: Explored implicit reasoning by distilling reasoning steps from a teacher model trained on explicit chain-of-thought reasoning, with reasoning happening vertically among hidden states in different layers rather than horizontally through intermediate words. Experiments conducted on multi-digit multiplication and grade school math problems.

**Key findings**:
- Implicit reasoning via hidden states enables solving tasks previously not solvable without explicit chain-of-thought
- The approach achieves speed comparable to no chain-of-thought reasoning
- Reasoning happens vertically among hidden states in different layers rather than horizontally through word generation

**Limitations**: not stated in abstract

### Deng et al. (2024)

**Research question**: Can language models be taught to internalize explicit chain-of-thought reasoning steps while maintaining high performance?

**Methodology**: Proposed a method starting with a model trained for explicit CoT reasoning, then gradually removing intermediate steps and finetuning the model, allowing internalization of intermediate reasoning steps while simplifying the reasoning process.

**Key findings**:
- A GPT-2 Small model can solve 9-by-9 multiplication with up to 99% accuracy using the internalization method, whereas standard training cannot solve beyond 4-by-4 multiplication
- The method proves effective on larger language models like Mistral 7B, achieving over 50% accuracy on GSM8K without producing intermediate steps
- Models can internalize CoT steps while maintaining high performance in final outputs
- The gradual removal and finetuning process enables simplified reasoning processes

**Limitations**: not stated in abstract

### Hao et al. (2024)

**Research question**: Can large language models reason more effectively by operating in a continuous latent space rather than being constrained to the language space?

**Methodology**: Introduced Coconut (Chain of Continuous Thought), which utilizes the last hidden state of the LLM as a representation of the reasoning state and feeds it back as the next input embedding directly in the continuous space, enabling breadth-first search reasoning patterns rather than deterministic chain-of-thought paths.

**Key findings**:
- Continuous thoughts can encode multiple alternative next steps, enabling breadth-first search rather than committing to a single deterministic path
- Coconut outperforms traditional chain-of-thought on logical reasoning tasks that require substantial search during planning
- The latent reasoning paradigm achieves a better trade-off between accuracy and efficiency compared to CoT
- Most word tokens in language space primarily ensure textual coherence and are not essential for reasoning

**Limitations**: not stated in abstract

### Li et al. (2024)

**Research question**: What causes compositional reasoning failures in LLMs, and how can these failures be patched?

**Methodology**: Used Logit Lens and intervention experiments to dissect inner hidden states of LLMs, locating multi-head self-attention (MHSA) modules within middle layers critical for accurate generation and leveraging of implicit reasoning results. Developed CREME, a lightweight method to patch errors via editing the located MHSA modules.

**Key findings**:
- Most compositional reasoning failures stem from improperly generated or leveraged implicit reasoning results
- Implicit reasoning results surface within middle layers and play a causative role in shaping final explicit reasoning results
- MHSA modules within middle layers emerge as linchpins in accurate generation and leveraging of implicit reasoning results
- CREME effectively patches errors in compositional reasoning via editing MHSA modules

**Limitations**: not stated in abstract

### Muennighoff et al. (2025)

**Research question**: What is the simplest approach to achieve test-time scaling and strong reasoning performance in language models?

**Methodology**: Curated a small dataset s1K of 1,000 questions with reasoning traces based on difficulty, diversity, and quality criteria, and developed budget forcing to control test-time compute by forcibly terminating or lengthening the model's thinking process. Supervised finetuned Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct on s1K and equipped it with budget forcing.

**Key findings**:
- s1-32B exceeds o1-preview on competition math questions by up to 27% on MATH and AIME24
- Scaling s1-32B with budget forcing allows extrapolating performance from 50% to 57% on AIME24
- Budget forcing can lead the model to double-check answers and fix incorrect reasoning steps
- The model, data, and code are open-source

**Limitations**: not stated in abstract

### Su et al. (2025)

**Research question**: Can hybrid representation mixing latent discrete tokens with text tokens improve reasoning efficiency while maintaining performance?

**Methodology**: Proposed partially abstracting initial reasoning steps using latent discrete tokens generated by VQ-VAE to reduce reasoning trace length. Explored this in two scenarios: training from scratch for Keys-Finding Maze problem, and fine-tuning LLMs on hybrid data with extended vocabulary including unseen latent tokens, using a training procedure that randomly mixes latent and text tokens.

**Key findings**:
- Hybrid representation significantly reduces the length of reasoning traces
- The approach consistently outperforms baseline methods in various benchmarks
- Random mixing of latent and text tokens enables fast adaptation to new latent tokens
- Many words in chain-of-thought data support textual coherence rather than core reasoning information

**Limitations**: not stated in abstract

### Yang et al. (2024)

**Research question**: Do large language models latently perform multi-hop reasoning when processing complex prompts that require identifying bridge entities and using knowledge about them?

**Methodology**: Analyzed two hops of latent reasoning individually: testing if indirectly mentioning the bridge entity increases the LLM's internal recall of that entity (first hop), and testing if increasing this recall causes the LLM to better utilize its knowledge about the bridge entity (second hop). Co-occurrence of both hops is considered indicative of latent multi-hop reasoning.

**Key findings**:
- Strong evidence of latent multi-hop reasoning exists for prompts of certain relation types, with the reasoning pathway used in more than 80% of prompts
- Utilization of latent reasoning is highly contextual and varies across different types of prompts
- Evidence for the second hop and full multi-hop traversal is moderate on average, while evidence for the first hop is substantial
- Clear scaling trend exists with increasing model size for the first hop but not for the second hop

**Limitations**: The utilization of latent reasoning is highly contextual and varies across prompt types, suggesting challenges in consistent multi-hop reasoning application.
