# Systematic Literature Review: Open World Detection

**Date**: 2026-10-26
**Papers surveyed**: 10
**Scope**: arXiv query "open world detection", category cs.CV, published between 2023-08-22 and 2025-08-21
**Citation format**: APA 7th edition

## Executive Summary

The field of open world detection has evolved significantly between 2023 and 2025, with research spanning multiple approaches to handle unknown objects in visual recognition systems. The surveyed literature reveals three dominant paradigms: (1) leveraging vision-language models and synthetic data generation to expand detection capabilities beyond fixed vocabularies, (2) developing unified architectures that support flexible prompting and multi-task perception, and (3) addressing practical deployment challenges through automated annotation pipelines and semi-supervised learning. Collectively, these works demonstrate a shift from purely appearance-based detection toward systems that integrate linguistic understanding, geometric reasoning, and hierarchical knowledge representation. The field shows strong convergence on the need to reduce manual annotation costs while improving generalization to novel object categories, particularly in domains like autonomous driving and aerial imagery where long-tailed distributions are prevalent.

## Methodology

This review surveyed 10 arXiv papers retrieved on 2026-10-26 using the query "open world detection", filtered to category cs.CV, published between 2023-08-22 and 2025-08-21. Papers were sorted by relevance and the top 10 were included. Metadata extraction (research question, methodology, key findings, limitations) was performed by language-model agents, with cross-paper synthesis performed by the lead agent.

**Limitations of this review**: arXiv preprints are not peer-reviewed; some included papers may not reflect their final published form. Coverage is limited to arXiv — papers published directly in venues without arXiv preprints are not represented.

## Themes

### Theme 1: Vision-Language Integration for Open-Vocabulary Detection

A dominant trend across the surveyed literature is the integration of vision-language models (VLMs) to enable detection beyond fixed class vocabularies. Kong et al. (2024) pioneered the use of synthetic captions generated by pre-trained VLMs to enrich object descriptions, introducing HyperLearner which bootstraps dense synthetic captions without expensive manual annotation. This approach was extended by Guan et al. (2025) with Prompt-DINO, which employs early fusion mechanisms to unify text prompts, visual prompts, and backbone features at the encoding stage, enabling deeper cross-modal interactions that resolve semantic ambiguities.

The most significant advancement in this direction comes from Ren et al. (2024) with DINO-X, a unified vision model pre-trained on Grounding-100M with over 100 million grounding samples. DINO-X supports text, visual, and customized prompts simultaneously, achieving state-of-the-art zero-shot detection performance with 56.0 AP on COCO and 63.3 AP on rare LVIS classes. Wei et al. (2025) extended this paradigm to aerial imagery with the OS-W2S Label Engine, constructing the MI-OAD dataset containing 2 million image-caption pairs—approximately 40 times larger than comparable datasets—which enabled Grounding DINO to achieve +31.1 AP50 improvement under zero-shot transfer.

These works collectively demonstrate that linguistic grounding provides a scalable mechanism for open-world detection, with performance gains proportional to the scale and diversity of training data.

### Theme 2: Automated Annotation and Data Engine Approaches

A second major theme addresses the practical challenge of annotation costs in open-world settings. Multiple papers propose automated pipelines that reduce or eliminate human intervention in dataset creation. Liang et al. (2024) introduced AIDE (Automatic Data Engine), which automatically identifies issues, curates data, improves models through auto-labeling, and verifies perception systems for autonomous driving in an iterative self-improvement loop. This approach directly addresses the long-tailed distribution problem where rare or unseen object categories challenge deployed perception models.

Rocky and Wu (2025) developed SAM2Auto, combining SMART-OD (open-world object detection with automatic mask generation) and FLASH (multi-object real-time video instance segmentation) to achieve comparable accuracy to manual annotation while dramatically reducing annotation time. Their system handles diverse datasets without retraining, employing statistical approaches to minimize false positives. Similarly, Yu et al. (2025) built LAVA for traffic video analytics, integrating video-specific open-world detection with multi-armed bandit sampling and long-term trajectory extraction, achieving 9.6× faster processing than the most accurate baseline.

These works share a common insight: automated annotation pipelines are essential for scaling open-world detection to real-world deployment scenarios where manual curation is infeasible.

### Theme 3: Geometric and Hierarchical Learning Strategies

Beyond vision-language integration, several papers explore structural learning approaches that leverage geometric cues or hierarchical knowledge representation. Yavuz and Güney (2024) proposed O1O, which groups known classes into superclasses to identify unknown objects through an odd-one-out scoring mechanism. This approach leverages geometric cues in addition to appearance-based features, achieving improved unknown detection without compromising known class performance through better partitioning of the feature space.

Kong et al. (2024) introduced hyperbolic vision-language learning to impose hierarchy between visual and caption embeddings, successfully mitigating noise from hallucination in synthetic captions. This geometric approach to representation learning addresses a critical limitation of VLM-based methods: the tendency for synthetic captions to introduce hallucinated descriptions that degrade detection quality.

These geometric approaches complement vision-language methods by providing structural constraints that improve generalization to novel categories.

### Theme 4: Semi-Supervised and Active Learning Formulations

The literature also addresses deployment realism through semi-supervised and active learning formulations. Mullappilly et al. (2024) introduced SS-OWOD (Semi-Supervised Open-World Detection), casting incremental learning stages in a semi-supervised manner to reduce reliance on human oracles. Their SS-OWFormer detector utilizes feature-alignment between original and augmented images, plus pseudo-labeling for unknown detection, demonstrating that state-of-the-art OWOD performance deteriorates dramatically without semi-supervised adaptations.

Chen et al. (2023) extended active learning to 3D object detection with Open-CRB, proposing Open Label Conciseness (OLC) to mine novel 3D objects with minimal annotation costs. Their framework supports comprehensive experimentation across multiple detectors and datasets, demonstrating that any generic active learning policy can be integrated with OLC to address open-world 3D detection with just a single round of selection.

These formulations recognize that real-world deployment requires continuous adaptation to novel categories without exhaustive manual labeling.

## Convergences and Disagreements

**Convergences**: The surveyed literature shows strong agreement on several points. First, all papers acknowledge that fixed-vocabulary detection is insufficient for real-world deployment, where long-tailed distributions and novel object categories are inevitable. Second, there is consensus that reducing manual annotation costs is critical—whether through automated pipelines (Liang et al., 2024; Rocky & Wu, 2025), synthetic data generation (Kong et al., 2024; Guan et al., 2025), or semi-supervised learning (Mullappilly et al., 2024). Third, multiple papers demonstrate that pre-training on large-scale grounding data significantly improves zero-shot transfer performance (Ren et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2025).

**Disagreements**: A notable tension exists between approaches that rely on pseudo-labeling versus those that avoid it. Yavuz and Güney (2024) note that additional supervision from pseudo-labels introduces confusion for known classes, causing notable performance decline. In contrast, Mullappilly et al. (2024) propose pseudo-labeling as a core mechanism for unknown detection, suggesting that the effectiveness depends on implementation specifics—particularly whether feature-alignment schemes are employed to stabilize representations. Additionally, while most papers focus on 2D detection, Chen et al. (2023) demonstrate that 3D point cloud detection presents unique challenges requiring specialized active learning strategies, suggesting domain-specific adaptations may be necessary.

## Gaps and Open Questions

Several gaps emerge from the collective literature. First, **limitations are rarely stated explicitly** in abstracts—8 of 10 papers reported "not stated in abstract" for limitations, suggesting either space constraints or insufficient critical reflection on failure modes. Second, while multiple papers address autonomous driving (Liang et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025) and aerial imagery (Wei et al., 2025), there is limited cross-domain evaluation, leaving open the question of whether methods generalize across vastly different visual domains.

Third, the computational costs of large-scale pre-training and vision-language integration are not systematically analyzed—DINO-X's Grounding-100M pre-training and Prompt-DINO's 0.5B synthetic instances raise questions about accessibility for resource-constrained settings. Finally, temporal consistency in video-based open-world detection remains underexplored; while Rocky and Wu (2025) and Yu et al. (2025) address video scenarios, most methods focus on static images, leaving open challenges in maintaining consistent object identity across frames when novel categories appear.

## Per-Paper Annotations

### Chen et al. (2023)

**Research question**: How can active learning methods for 3D object detection be extended to handle streaming point clouds that may contain unknown or novel objects in real-world deployments?
**Methodology**: The paper proposes Open Label Conciseness (OLC), a strategy that mines novel 3D objects with minimal annotation costs, and integrates it with the CRB active learning method to create the Open-CRB framework. Evaluated using 15 baseline methods, 2 types of 3D detectors (SECOND and PV-RCNN), and 3 benchmark datasets (KITTI, nuScenes, and Waymo).
**Key findings**:
- OLC successfully adapts 3D detection models to open world scenarios with just a single round of selection
- Any generic active learning policy can be integrated with OLC to efficiently address the OWAL-3D problem
- Open-CRB demonstrates superiority and flexibility in recognizing both novel and known classes with very limited labeling costs compared to state-of-the-art baselines
- The framework supports comprehensive experimentation with multiple baseline methods, detector types, and datasets
**Limitations**: Not stated in abstract

### Guan et al. (2025)

**Research question**: How can early fusion mechanisms and order-aligned query selection address limitations in late-stage feature fusion and suboptimal query selection for hybrid prompts open-world segmentation?
**Methodology**: The authors propose Prompt-DINO featuring three innovations: (1) early fusion mechanism unifying text/visual prompts and backbone features at initial encoding stage, (2) order-aligned query selection for DETR-based architectures optimizing structural alignment between text and visual queries, and (3) a generative data engine powered by RAP model synthesizing 0.5B diverse training instances through dual-path cross-verification.
**Key findings**:
- Prompt-DINO achieves state-of-the-art performance on open-world detection benchmarks
- Early fusion enables deeper cross-modal interactions that resolve semantic ambiguities
- The generative data engine reduces label noise by 80.5% compared to conventional approaches
- The framework significantly expands semantic coverage beyond fixed-vocabulary constraints
- Order-aligned query selection enhances semantic-spatial consistency during decoding
**Limitations**: Not stated in abstract

### Kong et al. (2024)

**Research question**: How can knowledge from vision-language models be transferred to enrich open-vocabulary descriptions automatically for open-world detection without expensive manual annotation?
**Methodology**: The authors propose bootstrapping dense synthetic captions using pre-trained vision-language models to provide rich descriptions on different image regions, and incorporate these captions to train a novel detector called HyperLearner. To mitigate noise from hallucination in synthetic captions, they introduce a hyperbolic vision-language learning approach that imposes a hierarchy between visual and caption embeddings.
**Key findings**:
- HyperLearner consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods including GLIP, GLIPv2, and Grounding DINO when using the same backbone
- The hyperbolic vision-language learning approach successfully mitigates noise caused by hallucination in synthetic captions
- Synthetic captions from VLMs can effectively replace expensive manual annotated caption datasets for training open-world detectors
- The model generalizes well to novel concepts across multiple benchmarks including COCO, LVIS, Object Detection in the Wild, and RefCOCO
**Limitations**: Not stated in abstract

### Liang et al. (2024)

**Research question**: How can vision-language and large language models be leveraged to automatically identify issues, curate data, improve models, and verify perception systems for autonomous driving without significant human effort?
**Methodology**: The authors design an Automatic Data Engine (AIDE) that automatically identifies issues, efficiently curates data, improves the model through auto-labeling, and verifies the model through generation of diverse scenarios. The process operates iteratively, allowing for continuous self-improvement of the model.
**Key findings**:
- AIDE demonstrates superior performance at reduced cost compared to existing learning paradigms on open-world detection benchmarks for autonomous driving datasets
- The iterative process enables continuous self-improvement of perception models without manual data curation
- The approach addresses the long-tailed distribution problem where rare or unseen object categories pose challenges to deployed perception models
- A comprehensive benchmark for open-world detection on AV datasets was established to evaluate various learning paradigms
**Limitations**: Not stated in abstract

### Mullappilly et al. (2024)

**Research question**: How can open-world object detection be made more realistic for real-world deployment by reducing reliance on human oracles during incremental learning?
**Methodology**: The authors introduce a semi-supervised open-world detection (SS-OWOD) formulation that casts incremental learning stages in a semi-supervised manner. They propose SS-OWFormer, a novel detector utilizing feature-alignment to align object query representations between original and augmented images, plus a pseudo-labeling scheme for unknown detection.
**Key findings**:
- State-of-the-art OWOD detector performance dramatically deteriorates in the proposed SS-OWOD setting
- SS-OWFormer's feature-alignment scheme effectively leverages large unlabeled and few labeled data
- The pseudo-labeling scheme exploits decoder object queries' inherent capability to capture object-specific information
- Experiments on 4 datasets (MS COCO, PASCAL, Objects365, DOTA) demonstrate the approach's effectiveness
**Limitations**: Not stated in abstract

### Ren et al. (2024)

**Research question**: How can a unified vision model achieve superior open-world object detection performance while supporting flexible prompting and multiple perception tasks?
**Methodology**: DINO-X employs a Transformer-based encoder-decoder architecture supporting text, visual, and customized prompts, including a universal object prompt for prompt-free detection. The model was pre-trained on Grounding-100M, a dataset with over 100 million grounding samples, and integrates multiple perception heads for detection, segmentation, pose estimation, captioning, and QA.
**Key findings**:
- DINO-X Pro achieves 56.0 AP on COCO zero-shot object detection benchmark
- Scores 59.8 AP and 52.4 AP on LVIS-minival and LVIS-val zero-shot benchmarks respectively
- Achieves 63.3 AP and 56.5 AP on rare classes of LVIS-minival and LVIS-val, improving previous SOTA by 5.8 AP and 5.0 AP
- Demonstrates significantly improved capacity for recognizing long-tailed objects
- Supports multiple perception tasks simultaneously including detection, segmentation, pose estimation, captioning, and QA
**Limitations**: Not stated in abstract

### Rocky and Wu (2025)

**Research question**: How can automated annotation pipelines address the scarcity of annotated datasets for vision-language models without human intervention or dataset-specific training?
**Methodology**: The authors introduce SAM2Auto, a fully automated annotation pipeline consisting of SMART-OD (combining automatic mask generation with open-world object detection) and FLASH (a multi-object real-time video instance segmentation system that maintains consistent object identification across video frames). The system employs statistical approaches to minimize detection errors while ensuring consistent object tracking.
**Key findings**:
- SAM2Auto achieves comparable accuracy to manual annotation while dramatically reducing annotation time and eliminating labor costs
- The system successfully handles diverse datasets without requiring retraining or extensive parameter adjustments
- Statistical approaches effectively minimize false positives compared to existing open-world detection methods requiring frame-specific hyperparameter tuning
- FLASH maintains consistent object identification across video frames even with intermittent detection gaps
- The pipeline provides a practical solution for large-scale dataset creation to accelerate VLM development
**Limitations**: Not stated in abstract

### Wei et al. (2025)

**Research question**: How can large-scale language-guided open-set aerial detection datasets be constructed to support fine-grained open-world detection beyond vocabulary-level descriptions?
**Methodology**: The authors propose the OS-W2S Label Engine, an automatic annotation pipeline centered around an open-source large vision-language model, integrating image-operation-based preprocessing with BERT-based postprocessing to handle diverse scene annotations across three language guidance levels (words, phrases, sentences).
**Key findings**:
- MI-OAD dataset contains 163,023 images and 2 million image-caption pairs, approximately 40 times larger than comparable datasets
- Training on MI-OAD lifts Grounding DINO by +31.1 AP50 and +34.7 Recall@10 with sentence-level inputs under zero-shot transfer
- MI-OAD pre-training yields state-of-the-art performance on multiple open-vocabulary aerial detection and remote sensing visual grounding benchmarks
- The dataset addresses limitations of current remote sensing grounding data
**Limitations**: Not stated in abstract

### Yavuz and Güney (2024)

**Research question**: How can geometric cues and class grouping strategies improve unknown object detection in open-world settings without compromising performance on known classes?
**Methodology**: The authors propose grouping known classes into superclasses to identify similarities between classes within a superclass, enabling unknown class identification through an odd-one-out scoring mechanism. This approach leverages geometric cues in addition to appearance-based features.
**Key findings**:
- Geometric cues improve unknown recall in open-world object detection
- Grouping known classes into superclasses achieves significant improvements in unknown recall consistently across all tasks
- The method achieves improved unknown detection without compromising known class performance through better partitioning of the feature space
- The odd-one-out scoring mechanism effectively identifies unknown classes by leveraging similarities within superclasses
**Limitations**: Additional supervision from pseudo-labels introduces confusion for known classes, causing notable decline in model performance for detecting known objects in the presence of noisy pseudo-labels

### Yu et al. (2025)

**Research question**: How can flexible and efficient querying of high-volume traffic video data be enabled through natural language rather than rigid SQL-based patterns?
**Methodology**: The authors build LAVA, a system with three components: (1) multi-armed bandit-based sampling for video segment-level localization, (2) video-specific open-world detection for object-level retrieval, and (3) long-term object trajectory extraction for temporal association. They developed a novel benchmark with diverse natural language predicates and fine-grained annotations.
**Key findings**:
- LAVA improves F1-scores for selection queries by 14% compared to baselines
- Reduces MPAE for aggregation queries by 0.39
- Achieves top-k precision of 86%
- Processes videos 9.6× faster than the most accurate baseline
**Limitations**: Not stated in abstract
